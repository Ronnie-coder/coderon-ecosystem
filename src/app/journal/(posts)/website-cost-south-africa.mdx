Ask five web designers what a website costs and you will get five different answers. Here is a plain-English guide to what small businesses in South Africa actually pay in 2026, and what you get for the money.

## The short answer

For a small business website built by a person (not a DIY builder), most South African businesses pay:

- **One-page website:** about **R1,500 to R5,000** once-off
- **3 to 5 page website:** about **R5,000 to R15,000** once-off
- **6 to 10+ pages, with a blog, booking or a small shop:** about **R15,000 to R60,000** once-off

There are also **monthly websites**, usually around **R250 to R1,000 a month**, where you pay nothing upfront but keep paying for as long as the site is live.

These ranges come from the public price pages of South African agencies and freelancers we looked at in October 2026.

## What makes one website cost more than another

1. **Number of pages.** Every page needs writing, design and testing.
2. **Custom design or a template.** Templates are cheaper. Custom design fits your brand and is usually lighter and faster.
3. **Features.** Online booking, a shop, a client login or a language switcher all add work.
4. **Who writes the words.** If you supply the text and photos, it costs less.
5. **Google set-up.** Some quotes include page titles, descriptions, a sitemap and Google Search Console. Some do not. Ask.

## Once-off or monthly?

**Once-off** means you pay to build the site and then only pay for hosting, your domain and any changes. Over two or three years it is usually cheaper, and you own the site.

**Monthly** spreads the cost and often includes hosting and small changes. It is easier on cash flow, but check the contract: who owns the website if you stop paying?

## Hidden costs to ask about

- **Domain name:** a .co.za costs roughly R100 to R150 a year.
- **Hosting:** from about R40 to R400 a month, depending on the provider and plan.
- **Business email:** sometimes included with hosting, sometimes extra.
- **Changes after launch:** is there a free fix period? What does a change cost after that?
- **Ownership:** make sure the domain is registered in your name.
