**Eddie's Cut** is a hair salon in Hellerup, Denmark, with more than 25 years of experience. Their reputation was excellent. Their website was not keeping up.

## The problem: slow, sending people away, and only found in one area

- On a phone the site was slow: Google PageSpeed gave it **47 out of 100** for mobile performance.
- The booking button opened a separate booking site, so people left the salon's website to book.
- The salon wanted to reach clients from nearby areas like Gentofte and Charlottenlund, but the site only really talked about Hellerup.

## What we did

1. **A redesign in Next.js.** A calm, premium look, clear prices by category (cuts, colour, care, beard) and a smooth menu that follows you as you scroll.
2. **Booking inside the website.** The booking system now opens within the site, so clients book without leaving.
3. **A page for each treatment.** Every service has its own page with the questions people actually ask. The homepage links to them in the text, so both visitors and Google can find them.
4. **Pages for nearby areas.** "Frisør Gentofte" and "Frisør Charlottenlund" pages speak to clients in the areas the salon wanted to grow in.
5. **Local SEO basics.** Local business schema, Google Maps on the contact section, opening hours, and titles and descriptions on every page.

## The result

On the same Google Lighthouse mobile test, the new site scores:

- **Performance: 92** (was 47)
- **Accessibility: 100** (was 88)
- **Best Practices: 100**
- **SEO: 100** (was 92)

The salon now has a website that loads quickly on a phone, keeps people on the page when they book, and talks to clients across Hellerup, Gentofte and Charlottenlund. See the before and after screenshots in the [Eddie's Cut case study](/playroom/eddies-cut/).
